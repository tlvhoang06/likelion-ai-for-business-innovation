ResiLend: Shared Parametric Trigger and AI-Assisted Triage Infrastructure for Post-Disaster Agricultural Debt Relief

Multi-Hazard Remote Sensing, Deterministic Regulatory Rules, and Tool-Grounded AI Operations for Rural Credit Resilience in Vietnam
Trần Văn Nhân · Lê Hoài Tuấn Khang · Trương Lê Vũ Hoàng · Nguyễn Đức Nguyên
Team EK15 — Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam · November 2026
Interactive Web MVP (2,895 Communes): https://resilend-demo.vercel.app
Empirical Dataset: Typhoon Yagi (06–15 Sep 2024) · 12 Northern Provinces (GADM v4.1)
Abstract

Natural disasters represent a recurring systemic credit shock in Vietnam, inflicting 91,622 billion VND in economic losses in 2024 and 104,732 billion VND in 2025; Typhoon Yagi (September 2024) alone affected approximately 190,000 billion VND in outstanding loans. Although Vietnam provides legal mechanisms for agricultural debt restructuring under Decree 55/2015/NĐ-CP (amended by Decree 156/2025/NĐ-CP) and Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN (commercial banks and People's Credit Funds; excluding policy banks such as VBSP per Article 2), implementation remains manual, household-by-household, and constrained by a strict window requiring restructuring completion while the loan is current or under 10 days overdue (before Group 2 classification). For non-agricultural loans, Circular 53/2024/TT-NHNN required 88 days after Typhoon Yagi to establish debt-group retention guidelines. We present ResiLend, a shared B2B FinTech and AI operational platform that converts open meteorological, hydrological, and satellite observations into a standardized commune-level trigger signal (GID_3) distributed via Open API (Circular 64/2024/TT-NHNN). ResiLend combines an auditable deterministic rule engine (R1 extreme rain, R2 resolved 2,895-commune river stage crosswalk, R3 typhoon wind) with a zero-training Google Gemini Function-Calling Operations Copilot that performs natural-language spatial filtering, staffing-constrained branch triage scheduling, and a structured dossier drafting workflow for local authorities (UBND). Evaluated retrospectively across 2,895 communes in 12 Northern provinces against UNOSAT 3966 and Sentinel-1 SAR flood observations, ResiLend cleanly separates Stage-1 Early Alerting (91.23% UNOSAT recall / 27.35% precision at −1.50h median lead; routing 1,338 R1|R2 Priority Review and 1,354 R3-only Watchlist communes) from Stage-2 Post-Event Flood Confirmation (Sentinel-1 + R2 + R1 + HAND 4-fold cross-validation: 63.46% precision / 79.11% recall, CV scripts and fold predictions published in submission_data/06_SUPPLEMENTARY_POST_EVENT/) and a proposed batch UBND verification gating 1-Tap deferral dispatch (subject to partner bank and local authority acceptance)—enabling banks to break even when avoiding Group 2 downgrade (5% potential specific risk provision under Decree 86/2024/NĐ-CP) on just 1 in 725 monitored loans.

Keywords: Agricultural Credit Relief, Parametric Disaster Triggers, Function-Calling LLM Agent, Remote Sensing (UNOSAT / Sentinel-1), Debt Group 1 Retention, Open Banking API, Vietnam.

1. Problem Statement & Institutional Bottlenecks

1.1 The Post-Disaster Credit Bottleneck in Vietnam

When severe typhoons and floods hit rural Vietnam, farming households simultaneously lose harvest income and face fixed loan repayment deadlines. According to the General Statistics Office (GSO), natural disasters caused 91,622 billion VND in economic losses in 2024 and 104,732 billion VND in 2025. Typhoon Yagi (landfall 07 September 2024) illustrates the discovery lag across the banking system: two weeks after landfall (~21–22 September 2024), credit institutions had identified roughly 83,000 affected customers holding 116,000 billion VND in affected debt; three months later (December 2024), the sector-wide tally rose by roughly two-thirds to 94,000 customers and 190,000 billion VND. At Agribank alone, mid-September 2024 estimates grew from 12,000 borrowers (21,000 billion VND) to over 28,000 borrowers (~40,000 billion VND) as isolated communes re-established contact. Agricultural and rural trade households bear the brunt of this shock—accounting for 97.2% of affected borrowers in provinces such as Lào Cai.

When repayment extensions are not completed while loans remain under 10 days overdue (Group 1 · Performing Loans), automatic credit downgrades penalize both parties: under Circular 31/2024/TT-NHNN, loans overdue from 10 to 90 days migrate to Group 2 (special mention / attention-needed—prior to Group 3–5 non-performing bad debt), impairing borrower credit records while requiring commercial banks to book potential specific risk provisions of 5% for Group 2 and 20% for Group 3 under Decree 86/2024/NĐ-CP (not a realized loss or penalty).

1.2 Structural Causes of Operational Delay

  1. Paper-Bound, Household-by-Household Processing Before Reaching 10 Days Overdue: Under Decree 55/2015/NĐ-CP (amended through Decree 156/2025/NĐ-CP) and Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN (Article 2, Article 3 Clause 1, and Article 8 Clause 2, effective 15 November 2025, replacing Circulars 10/2015 and 25/2018 for commercial banks and People's Credit Funds, excluding VBSP), credit institutions may restructure agricultural repayment schedules (up to 12 months for short-term loans and 36 months for medium- and long-term loans) and retain the borrower's pre-restructuring debt group when restructuring is processed while the obligation is current or under 10 days overdue (before Group 2 migration under Circular 31/2024), supported by a borrower request and bank assessment. Debt freezing further relies on disaster damage confirmation certified by commune authorities (UBND). Inundated roads and disrupted communications make collecting individual paper dossiers across tens of thousands of households unfeasible within this window.
  2. Ad-Hoc Regulatory Cycles Outside Agriculture: For non-agricultural rural loans, each major catastrophe requires a separate regulatory document. Following Typhoon Yagi, Circular 53/2024/TT-NHNN (effective 04 December 2024) took 88 days to be issued. Subsequent flood events between July and October 2025 relied on Official Dispatch 9651/NHNN-TD (04 November 2025), directing credit institutions to apply existing procedures.
  3. Post-Storm Information Asymmetry: Even when banks proactively waive branch visits, credit officers lack real-time, commune-level visibility into which loan portfolios are physically flooded, forcing manual household-by-household tallying across weeks while the 10-day Group 1 clock continues to run.
  4. Limited Risk Transfer to Agricultural Insurance: From 2020 to 2025, cumulative agricultural insurance premiums in Vietnam totaled only 271.3 billion VND—equivalent to 0.06% of non-life insurance premiums—across more than 9 million farming households. In response, the Government promulgated Decree 346/2026/NĐ-CP (signed 05 September 2026, effective 20 October 2026, amending Decree 58/2018/NĐ-CP), introducing weather-index and remote-sensing index insurance with a 15-business-day payout requirement and raising state premium subsidies up to 95% for poor/near-poor households, 50% for other farmers, and 30% for agricultural enterprises. However, as of November 2026, no remote-sensing index policy has been commercially launched and existing insurance products remain decoupled from bank loan repayment schedules.

2. System Architecture, Hybrid AI & FinTech Integration

2.1 Core Shared Infrastructure Modules

ResiLend operates as a neutral, multi-tenant disaster trigger infrastructure connecting open hydro-meteorological and satellite observations with commercial banks, commune authorities, insurers, and the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) through four core modules:

  • Module 1 — Standardized Commune Trigger Engine: Processes hourly rainfall (GSMaP v8), tropical cyclone wind fields (IBTrACS), resolved national river gauge basin crosswalks (R2), and post-event satellite flood masks (Sentinel-1 SAR + HAND) to emit standardized Stage-1 Early Alert and Stage-2 Confirmation JSON signals per GID_3 commune.
  • Module 2 — Commune-Level Remote-Sensing Loss Dossier for Proposed Batch UBND Attestation: Generates an area-level flood extent, rainfall, and wind evidence report designed as a proposed batch attestation for the Commune People's Committee (UBND) to review and endorse at commune scale—aimed at streamlining repetitive household-by-household confirmations, subject to partner bank and local authority acceptance.
  • Module 3 — Two-Stage Routing (Early Alert Tracks → ML-Confirmed 1-Tap & Exception Queue): In Stage 1, participating banks match triggered GID_3 codes internally and split R1|R2 communes (1,338) into Priority Review and R3-only communes (1,354) into Watchlist for early readiness. In Stage 2, post-event HistGradientBoostingClassifier confirmation (fusing Sentinel-1 + R2 + R1 + HAND ≤ 10m across 2,540 classifiable communes while keeping 355 outside-scan communes strictly null) and the proposed batch UBND loss attestation gate pre-filled 1-Tap deferral requests before loans reach 10 days overdue under Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN, while recovered Watchlist/missed pockets and out-of-scan communes enter the Gemini Exception Queue and counter paper channel.
  • Module 4 — Multi-Party Open API for Parametric Insurance & SBV Supervision: Distributes the verified commune event payload via REST/OAuth 2.0 Open API (Circular 64/2024/TT-NHNN) to trigger index insurance payouts under Decree 346/2026/NĐ-CP (15-business-day payout SLA) and provide live provincial risk summaries for SBV oversight.

2.2 Three-Layer Hybrid AI & Geospatial Architecture

In regulated banking, uncalibrated black-box models cannot autonomously approve or deny loan restructuring, nor can post-event satellite models replace Stage-1 early alerting before multi-year partner loss records exist. ResiLend therefore cleanly separates deterministic Day-1 triggers, live Stage-2 ML confirmation plus Zero-PII Gemini triage, and Phase-2 cross-storm calibration across three layers:

  1. Layer 1 — Deterministic Statutory Trigger & Heuristic Exception Ranking (Stage 1 Early Alerting): Objective legal thresholds (R1 rainfall >200 mm, R2 river stage >BĐ3 across 6 basins, and R3 R50/R64 storm wind) route 1,338 communes into Priority Review (R1|R2) and 1,354 communes into Watchlist (R350-only) at an invariant -1.50h median lead (27.35% Prec / 91.23% Rec). Alongside binary rules, an auditable 0–100 heuristic score ranks outside-scan pockets and exception queues using four additive weights: satellite flood ratio (≤38 pts), near-threshold rain (≤28 pts), wind swath (≤18 pts), and basin flood density + exposure (≤16 pts).
  2. Layer 2 — Live Dual AI Engine: Stage-2 Post-Event ML Confirmation + Zero-PII Gemini Copilot (MVP + Backtest): ResiLend pairs two complementary, strictly bounded AI components without ever automating final credit decisions:
    • Stage-2 Supervised Flood Confirmation (HistGradientBoostingClassifier, 4-fold GroupKFold CV): Fuses post-event Sentinel-1 water fraction & coverage (08–20 Sep, pre-21 Sep composite window), river trigger R2, extreme rain R1, and terrain elevation (HAND ≤ 10m) across the 2,540 classifiable communes—achieving 63.46% precision and 79.11% recall (568 TP, 327 FP, 150 FN, 1,495 TN; 61.93% Prec / 81.34% Rec at fixed-88% sensitivity; full CV code, provincial fold splits, and commune-level predictions published in submission_data/06_SUPPLEMENTARY_POST_EVENT/) to triage Watchlist and missed-gauge pockets before 1-Tap offers. In operational backtesting, it recovers 256 / 321 (79.8%) flooded R3-only Watchlist communes (413 flagged) and 52 / 63 (82.5%) unflagged Stage-1 false negatives across Phú Thọ (59 FN) and Thái Nguyên (4 FN). Crucially, all 355 outside-UNOSAT communes remain strictly null (Unknown, never imputed to zero). Because post-event Sentinel-1 imagery spans 08–20 Sep 2024, the end-to-end <72h post-imagery turnaround remains a Phase 1 live pilot target rather than a proven retrospective runtime.
    • Zero-PII Google Gemini Function-Calling Copilot (gemini-2.5-flash): Binds 4 declared workspace tools in the public codebase over public GID_3, hazard, HAND ≤ 10m, and s2_ml_prob records only—never transmitting borrower PII or balances to the cloud: query_communes (supports workflow_track, require_s2_ml_confirm, min_ml_prob, min_hand_pct, sort_by: 's2_ml_prob', plus outside-scan isolation); plan_branch_triage (routes credit officers under staffing/hour caps across ML-confirmed Watchlist, rural FN pockets, and Priority communes); get_river_stations (queries peak water levels and BĐ3 timestamps across all 11 gauges); and dispatch_ui_action (highlights polygons and toggles S1/UNOSAT layers), paired with a client-side Dossier drafting workflow compiling cited commune evidence briefs (s2_ml_prob and HAND ≤ 10m) for proposed batch UBND attestation.
  3. Layer 3 — Phase 2 Multi-Storm Calibration & Multimodal Intake (2027–2028 Roadmap): Because Stage-2 CV was evaluated on held-out provinces within one Typhoon Yagi event using post-event SAR, cross-event calibration awaits multi-storm pilot records combining GloFAS hourly river discharge forecasts, hourly station hydrographs, anonymized multi-year bank Group 2+ delinquency labels, and multimodal intake (paper dossier OCR / field flood photo triage).

3. Empirical Backtest on Typhoon Yagi (September 2024)

3.1 Retrospective Dataset & Signal Coverage Across 2,895 Communes

We evaluated the ResiLend trigger pipeline across 2,895 communes in 12 Northern provinces impacted by Typhoon Yagi between 06 and 15 September 2024. The backtest design maintains three methodological boundaries: (i) strict separation between Stage-1 Early Alerting and Stage-2 Post-Event Flood Confirmation—hydro-meteorological thresholds (R1, R2, R3) route Priority/Watchlist readiness early (91.23% UNOSAT recall / 27.35% precision at -1.50h median lead), whereas experimental Sentinel-1 + R2 + R1 + HAND models confirm flood damage post-event (63.46% precision / 79.11% recall in 4-fold cross-validation) to gate 1-Tap dispatch; (ii) independent evaluation references—UNOSAT 3966 (12 September 2024 Red River Delta flood map) never leaks into early triggers; and (iii) explicit handling of missing coverage—the 355 communes outside the UNOSAT satellite swath remain marked as Unknown coverage rather than 0% flood.

3.2 Representative Commune Cases in the Backtest Dataset

Comparing meteorological triggers against satellite flood observations illustrates four operational cases available for interactive inspection in the Web MVP:

  • Priority Track & Post-Confirm 1-Tap Dispatch (Vũ Xá): Vũ Xá Commune (Lục Nam, Bắc Giang — VNM.3.4.26_1) recorded both extreme rainfall (210.45 mm, R1 = 1) and typhoon wind force (R3 = 1), corroborated post-event by 84.89% UNOSAT flood extent (85.2% HAND ≤10m) and 90.8% Stage-2 ML confirmation. In Stage 1 it enters the R1|R2 Priority Review queue immediately, and once Stage-2 ML and proposed batch UBND attestation confirm flood impact, opted-in borrowers unlock pre-filled 1-Tap deferral offers.
  • Resolved River Crosswalk Rescue (Điêu Lương): Điêu Lương Commune (Cẩm Khê, Phú Thọ — VNM.44.1.6_1) saw only 132.28 mm local rain (R1 = 0) and missed the storm wind swath (R3_R50 = 0), but crossed R2 = 1 via the Yên Bái basin crosswalk (52.2 km proximity, 76.01% HAND ≤ 10m) and recorded 22.19% UNOSAT inundation (78.9% Stage-2 ML)—showing how resolved R2 mapping expands Priority Review recall beyond local rain gauges.
  • Watchlist & Exception Queue Triage (Gia Thủy & Lệnh Khanh): Gia Thủy Commune (Nho Quan, Ninh Bình — VNM.42.4.6_1) experienced 76.12% UNOSAT inundation (99.7% HAND ≤ 10m, 88.6% Stage-2 ML) despite 166.19 mm local rain (R1 = 0) and lying outside the 6 mapped R2 basins (R2 = 0); it entered the R3_R50 Watchlist alongside Nam Phương Tiến (Chương Mỹ, Hà Nội — VNM.27.6.15_1) at 43.89% inundation. Likewise, unflagged pockets such as Lệnh Khanh (Hạ Hoà, Phú Thọ — VNM.44.3.16_1) missed all three Stage-1 rules but hit 63.85% UNOSAT inundation and are recovered via 94.6% Stage-2 ML inside the Gemini Exception Queue for officer review and proposed batch UBND attestation.
  • Out-of-Scan Satellite Blind Spot (Coverage Safeguard — Bạch Đằng): Bạch Đằng (Hạ Long, Quảng Ninh — VNM.49.7.1_1) recorded 237.07 mm rainfall (R1 = 1) and core typhoon winds (R3 = 1), but lies among the 355 communes outside the UNOSAT 3966 Red River Delta swath (PARTIAL_OR_NO_ANALYSIS_COVERAGE). Instead of coercing missing satellite data or CV labels to 0%, ResiLend preserves both its UNOSAT status as Outside UNOSAT Scan and its Stage-2 CV probability as null (Unknown) so coastal communes are never dropped due to satellite swath boundaries.

4.2 Revenue Model & Bank Break-Even Analysis (1 in 725 Loans)

ResiLend charges partner banks a one-time API integration fee plus an annual monitoring fee of 10,000 – 20,000 VND per monitored loan per year (generating ~0.5 – 2 billion VND/year with one pilot institution, scaling to 22 – 58 billion VND/year across the SAM), alongside per-policy signal fees from parametric insurers.

Under Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN and Circular 31/2024/TT-NHNN, an agricultural loan whose restructuring is not completed while under 10 days overdue migrates to Group 2 (attention-needed / special mention, not yet Group 3–5 bad debt), requiring a 5% specific risk provision under Decree 86/2024/NĐ-CP (calculated on outstanding balance net of deductible collateral). With an average rural loan exposure of 290 million VND (4.2 quadrillion VND ÷ 14.6 million borrowers), preventing a single unsecured Group 2 downgrade avoids up to 14.5 million VND in potential additional provisioning (not a realized loss or penalty). At a monitoring fee of 20,000 VND/loan/year:

Break-Even Ratio = 20,000 VND / (290,000,000 VND × 5%) = 20,000 / 14,500,000 = 1 in 725 monitored loans (0.138%) (Eq. 1)

For collateralized loans where deductible collateral reduces the net provisioned balance, the break-even threshold scales proportionally. Because satellite imagery is open and hydro-meteorological processing scales by geographic region rather than per individual borrower, marginal data cost per additional loan within a covered province is near zero, supporting long-term financial sustainability without relying on state subsidies. For commercial insurance triggers under Decree 346/2026/NĐ-CP, production feeds integrate certified observations from NCHMF, WMO, and the National Remote Sensing Department alongside 5-year website publication and audit retention.

No commercial remote-sensing product launched yet; purchased separately; does not alter loan due dates. Secondary B2B customer subscribing to ResiLend's 5-year audited index signal.
State Agency Data (NCHMF, National Remote Sensing Dept.) Authoritative sources recognized under Decree 346/2026. Raw meteorological/satellite observations; not mapped to commune GID_3 or bank loan books. Authoritative upstream data inputs ingested by ResiLend.
International Precedents (MiCRO Haiti, CRF-I Pakistan, WB Cat DDO) Validated disaster-linked finance structures internationally. MiCRO charges borrowers ~3% of loan value; CRF-I and Cat DDO act at MFI/sovereign level. International reference; ResiLend operates at loan level with 0 VND borrower fee.
