ResiLend: Shared Parametric Trigger and AI-Assisted Triage Infrastructure for Post-Disaster Agricultural Debt Relief

Multi-Hazard Remote Sensing, Deterministic Regulatory Rules, and Tool-Grounded AI Operations for Rural Credit Resilience in Vietnam
Trần Văn Nhân · Lê Hoài Tuấn Khang · Trương Lê Vũ Hoàng · Nguyễn Đức Nguyên
Team EK15 — Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam · November 2026
Interactive Web MVP (2,895 Communes): https://resilend-demo.vercel.app
Empirical Dataset: Typhoon Yagi (06–15 Sep 2024) · 12 Northern Provinces (GADM v4.1)
Reviewer Note: The Web MVP includes a built-in Local Tool Fallback engine, allowing immediate execution of commune filtering, officer dispatch scheduling, and UBND memo generation without requiring a personal Gemini API key (optional BYOK supported for live LLM chat).
Abstract

Natural disasters represent a recurring systemic credit shock in Vietnam, inflicting 91,622 billion VND in economic losses in 2024 and 104,732 billion VND in 2025; Typhoon Yagi (September 2024) alone affected approximately 190,000 billion VND in outstanding loans. Although Vietnam provides legal mechanisms for agricultural debt restructuring under Decree 55/2015/NĐ-CP (amended by Decree 156/2025/NĐ-CP) and Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN, implementation remains manual, household-by-household, and constrained by a strict window requiring borrower requests while current or at most 10 days overdue. For non-agricultural loans, Circular 53/2024/TT-NHNN required 88 days after Typhoon Yagi to establish debt-group retention guidelines. We present ResiLend, a shared B2B FinTech and AI operational platform that converts open meteorological, hydrological, and satellite observations into a standardized commune-level trigger signal (GID_3) distributed via Open API (Circular 64/2024/TT-NHNN). ResiLend combines an auditable deterministic rule engine (R1 extreme rain, R2 river stage, R3 typhoon wind) with a zero-training Google Gemini Function-Calling Operations Copilot that performs natural-language spatial filtering, staffing-constrained branch triage scheduling, and batch commune loss attestation drafting for local authorities (UBND). Evaluated retrospectively across 2,895 communes in 12 Northern provinces against UNOSAT 3966 and Sentinel-1 SAR flood observations, ResiLend demonstrates how a dual-track workflow—1-tap digital consent for clear triggers and AI-prioritized exception review for missed floods—enables banks to break even when avoiding Group 2 downgrade (5% provision under Decree 86/2024/NĐ-CP) on just 1 in 725 monitored loans.

Keywords: Agricultural Credit Relief, Parametric Disaster Triggers, Function-Calling LLM Agent, Remote Sensing (UNOSAT / Sentinel-1), Debt Group 1 Retention, Open Banking API, Vietnam.

1. Problem Statement & Institutional Bottlenecks

1.1 The Post-Disaster Credit Bottleneck in Vietnam

When severe typhoons and floods hit rural Vietnam, farming households simultaneously lose harvest income and face fixed loan repayment deadlines. According to the General Statistics Office (GSO), natural disasters caused 91,622 billion VND in economic losses in 2024 and 104,732 billion VND in 2025. Typhoon Yagi (landfall 07 September 2024) illustrates the discovery lag across the banking system: two weeks after landfall (~21–22 September 2024), credit institutions had identified roughly 83,000 affected customers holding 116,000 billion VND in affected debt; three months later (December 2024), the sector-wide tally rose by roughly two-thirds to 94,000 customers and 190,000 billion VND. At Agribank alone, mid-September 2024 estimates grew from 12,000 borrowers (21,000 billion VND) to over 28,000 borrowers (~40,000 billion VND) as isolated communes re-established contact. Agricultural and rural trade households bear the brunt of this shock—accounting for 97.2% of affected borrowers in provinces such as Lào Cai.

When repayment extensions are not completed before delinquency thresholds expire, automatic credit downgrades penalize both parties: under Circular 31/2024/TT-NHNN, loans overdue from 10 to 90 days migrate to Group 2 (special mention), impairing borrower credit histories while requiring commercial banks to book specific provisions of 5% for Group 2 and 20% for Group 3 under Decree 86/2024/NĐ-CP.

1.2 Structural Causes of Operational Delay

  1. Paper-Bound, Household-by-Household Processing Within a 10-Day Window: Under Decree 55/2015/NĐ-CP (amended through Decree 156/2025/NĐ-CP) and Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN (Article 3 Clause 1 and Article 8 Clause 2, effective 15 November 2025, replacing Circulars 10/2015 and 25/2018), credit institutions may restructure agricultural repayment schedules (up to 12 months for short-term loans and 36 months for medium- and long-term loans) and retain the borrower's pre-restructuring debt group only when the repayment obligation is current or at most 10 days overdue, supported by a borrower request and bank assessment. Debt freezing further requires individual damage minutes certified by commune authorities (UBND). Inundated roads and disrupted communications make collecting paper dossiers across tens of thousands of households unfeasible within 10 days.
  2. Ad-Hoc Regulatory Cycles Outside Agriculture: For non-agricultural rural loans, each major catastrophe requires a separate regulatory document. Following Typhoon Yagi, Circular 53/2024/TT-NHNN (effective 04 December 2024) took 88 days to be issued. Subsequent flood events between July and October 2025 relied on Official Dispatch 9651/NHNN-TD (04 November 2025), directing credit institutions to apply existing procedures.
  3. Post-Storm Information Asymmetry: Even when banks proactively waive branch visits, credit officers lack real-time, commune-level visibility into which loan portfolios are physically flooded, forcing manual household-by-household tallying across weeks while the 10-day delinquency clock continues to run.
  4. Limited Risk Transfer to Agricultural Insurance: From 2020 to 2025, cumulative agricultural insurance premiums in Vietnam totaled only 271.3 billion VND—equivalent to 0.06% of non-life insurance premiums—across more than 9 million farming households. In response, the Government promulgated Decree 346/2026/NĐ-CP (signed 05 September 2026, effective 20 October 2026, amending Decree 58/2018/NĐ-CP), introducing weather-index and remote-sensing index insurance with a 15-business-day payout requirement and raising state premium subsidies up to 95% for poor/near-poor households, 50% for other farmers, and 30% for agricultural enterprises. However, as of November 2026, no remote-sensing index policy has been commercially launched and existing insurance products remain decoupled from bank loan repayment schedules.

2. System Architecture, Hybrid AI & FinTech Integration

2.1 Core Shared Infrastructure Modules

ResiLend operates as a neutral, multi-tenant disaster trigger infrastructure connecting open hydro-meteorological and satellite observations with commercial banks, commune authorities, insurers, and the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) through four core modules:

  • Module 1 — Standardized Commune Trigger Engine: Processes hourly rainfall (GSMaP v8), tropical cyclone wind fields (IBTrACS), national river gauge peaks, and satellite flood masks (Sentinel-1 SAR) to emit a standardized JSON event signal indicating which commune (GID_3) crossed which statutory hazard rule, and at what timestamp.
  • Module 2 — Commune-Level Remote-Sensing Loss Dossier for Batch UBND Attestation: Generates an area-level flood extent, rainfall, and wind evidence report for the Commune People's Committee (UBND) to sign once for the entire commune, replacing repetitive household-by-household confirmations across all participating banks.
  • Module 3 — Dual-Track Bank Relief & AI-Assisted Exception Queue: Participating banks match triggered GID_3 codes against their internal loan records (linked with borrower opt-in at origination) and send pre-filled 1-Tap deferral requests via mobile banking app or authenticated SMS to record borrower requests within the 10-day window of Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN. Communes with single-rule alerts, missed rainfall triggers, or satellite coverage gaps are routed to a credit officer Exception Queue supported by a counter paper fallback channel.
  • Module 4 — Multi-Party Open API for Parametric Insurance & SBV Supervision: Distributes the verified commune event payload via REST/OAuth 2.0 Open API (Circular 64/2024/TT-NHNN) to trigger index insurance payouts under Decree 346/2026/NĐ-CP (15-business-day payout SLA) and provide live provincial risk summaries for SBV oversight.

2.2 Three-Layer Hybrid AI & Geospatial Architecture

In regulated banking, uncalibrated black-box models cannot be permitted to autonomously approve or deny loan restructuring before multi-year partner loss records exist. ResiLend therefore separates hazard detection, AI operational triage, and future threshold calibration into three distinct layers:

  1. Layer 1 — Deterministic Trigger & Heuristic Exception Ranking (Live in Backtest): Statutory weather thresholds (R1, R2, R3) determine primary commune alerts. For communes where local rain gauges miss downstream flooding (Rain < 200mm while satellite flood ratios are elevated or optical coverage is unavailable), an auditable rule-weighted heuristic ranks the Exception Queue on a 0–100 triage priority score weighted by four additive components: satellite flood coverage ratio (up to 38 pts), near-threshold 24h rainfall proximity (up to 28 pts), R50/R64 typhoon wind exposure (up to 18 pts), and provincial basin flood density and loan exposure concentration (up to 16 pts). This score orders manual review queues and is not treated as a calibrated machine-learning flood probability.
  2. Layer 2 — Zero-Training AI Operations Copilot via Google Gemini Function Calling (Live in Web MVP): ResiLend integrates a Google Gemini Function-Calling Agent (gemini-2.5-flash) bound to four deterministic workspace tools over the 2,895-commune dataset. Function-calling requests to the Google Gemini API transmit only administrative commune codes (GID_3) and public meteorological or remote-sensing indicators, never transmitting personal identifiable information (PII), customer identities, or individual borrower loan balances to the cloud language model:
    • query_communes: Converts natural-language officer queries into multi-criteria filters across all 2,895 communes (e.g., isolating communes outside the UNOSAT scan in Bắc Giang or flooded communes missed by R1 rain gauges), keeping missing satellite coverage as Unknown rather than 0%.
    • plan_branch_triage: Computes staffing-constrained field dispatch schedules by clustering priority communes by district (Huyện) under explicit officer headcount and time-window limits (e.g., "3 credit officers within 48 hours in Thái Bình") to maximize retained Group 1 loans and avoided 5% provisioning.
    • get_river_stations: Queries peak stage, Alert Level 3 (BĐ3) exceedances, and post-landfall lag hours across all 11 Northern hydrological gauges.
    • dispatch_ui_action: Controls the interactive Leaflet map—highlighting filtered commune polygons, zooming to target bounds (flyToBounds), switching timeline dates, and drafting structured Vietnamese/English submission memos (Tờ trình) for branch directors and UBND sign-off.
  3. Layer 3 — Phase 2 Machine Learning Threshold Calibration (Pilot Roadmap): As partner banks accumulate anonymized commune-level loan delinquency and loss records across storm seasons, supervised threshold calibration will refine local trigger cutoffs per micro-basin.

3. Empirical Backtest on Typhoon Yagi (September 2024)

3.1 Retrospective Dataset & Signal Coverage Across 2,895 Communes

We evaluated the ResiLend trigger pipeline across 2,895 communes in 12 Northern provinces impacted by Typhoon Yagi between 06 and 15 September 2024. The backtest design maintains two methodological boundaries: (i) separation of trigger inputs from evaluation references—meteorological observations (R1, R2, R3) act as operational triggers, whereas UNOSAT 3966 (12 September 2024 Red River Delta flood map) and Sentinel-1 SAR serve as post-event flood references; and (ii) explicit handling of missing coverage—the 355 communes outside the UNOSAT satellite swath remain marked as Unknown coverage rather than 0% flood.

3.2 Representative Commune Cases in the Backtest Dataset

Comparing meteorological triggers against satellite flood observations illustrates three operational cases available for interactive inspection in the Web MVP:

  • Dual-Rule Match (Track A — 1-Tap Auto-Eligibility): Vũ Xá Commune (Lục Nam, Bắc Giang — VNM.3.4.26_1) recorded both extreme rainfall (210.4 mm, R1 = 1) and typhoon wind force (R3 = 1), corroborated by 84.9% UNOSAT flood extent (alongside Trí Yên at 80.2%). Opted-in borrowers in these communes qualify immediately for pre-filled 1-Tap deferral offers.
  • Missed-Rainfall Flood Pocket (Track B — Exception Queue Priority): Gia Thủy Commune (Nho Quan, Ninh Bình — VNM.42.4.6_1) experienced 76.1% UNOSAT inundation (and 20.5% Sentinel-1 flood proxy) from upstream river overflow even though local 24h rainfall reached only 166.2 mm (below the 200 mm R1 cutoff). Similarly, Nam Phương Tiến (Chương Mỹ, Hà Nội — VNM.27.6.15_1) suffered 43.9% inundation with 130.8 mm local rainfall. Relying on rainfall thresholds alone would overlook these flooded communes; ResiLend routes them into the Exception Queue for batch UBND attestation and credit officer review.
  • Out-of-Scan Satellite Blind Spot (Coverage Safeguard): Bạch Đằng (Hạ Long, Quảng Ninh — VNM.49.7.1_1) recorded 237.1 mm rainfall (R1 = 1) and core typhoon winds (R3 = 1), but lies among the 355 communes outside the UNOSAT 3966 Red River Delta swath (PARTIAL_OR_NO_ANALYSIS_COVERAGE). Instead of coercing missing satellite data to 0% flood, ResiLend preserves its coverage status as Outside UNOSAT Scan so coastal communes are never dropped due to satellite swath boundaries.

4.2 Revenue Model & Bank Break-Even Analysis (1 in 725 Loans)

ResiLend charges partner banks a one-time API integration fee plus an annual monitoring fee of 10,000 – 20,000 VND per monitored loan per year (generating ~0.5 – 2 billion VND/year with one pilot institution, scaling to 22 – 58 billion VND/year across the SAM), alongside per-policy signal fees from parametric insurers.

Under Circular 29/2025/TT-NHNN and Circular 31/2024/TT-NHNN, an agricultural loan that misses the 10-day overdue restructuring window migrates to Group 2, requiring a 5% specific provision under Decree 86/2024/NĐ-CP (calculated on outstanding balance net of deductible collateral). With an average rural loan exposure of 290 million VND (4.2 quadrillion VND ÷ 14.6 million borrowers), preventing a single unsecured Group 2 downgrade avoids up to 14.5 million VND in new provisioning. At a monitoring fee of 20,000 VND/loan/year:

Break-Even Ratio = 20,000 VND / (290,000,000 VND × 5%) = 20,000 / 14,500,000 = 1 in 725 monitored loans (0.138%) (Eq. 1)

For collateralized loans where deductible collateral reduces the net provisioned balance, the break-even threshold scales proportionally. Because satellite imagery is open and hydro-meteorological processing scales by geographic region rather than per individual borrower, marginal data cost per additional loan within a covered province is near zero, supporting long-term financial sustainability without relying on state subsidies. For commercial insurance triggers under Decree 346/2026/NĐ-CP, production feeds integrate certified observations from NCHMF, WMO, and the National Remote Sensing Department alongside 5-year website publication and audit retention.
